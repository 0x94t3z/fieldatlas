HIST2041 Early Modern Europe - lecture 7 notes (wk 4, Tue)
Religion & the state: Augsburg -> Westphalia

- Lecturer: Dr Helmsley. reading for seminar: Wilson, Europe's Tragedy ch.1 + 22, primary source pack pp.14-19

AUGSBURG 1555
- Peace of Augsburg, 25 Sept 1555, betw Charles V's side (his brother Ferdinand negotiating) & the Lutheran princes, after the Schmalkaldic War + Princes' Revolt (1552)
- key principle: cuius regio, eius religio -> each prince picks Lutheran or Catholic for his territory
- ONLY those 2. Calvinists (Reformed) NOT included -> big problem later (Palatinate went Calvinist 1560s)
- ius emigrandi = subjects who disagreed could sell up & move w/o penalty. Helmsley: "a right to leave, not a right to believe"
- ecclesiastical reservation: if a Catholic prince-bishop converted he had to give up his lands -> Protestants never accepted this, source of disputes
- imperial cities: some mixed (Augsburg itself was biconfessional)

WHY IT BROKE DOWN
- Calvinism excluded, ecclesiastical reservation contested, Counter-Reformation pressure, Bohemia
- Defenestration of Prague 23 May 1618 -> start of Thirty Years War (1618-48)
- war devastated German lands; est. pop loss in some regions 1/3 or more (Helmsley stressed regional variation, Wurttemberg much worse than NW)

WESTPHALIA 1648
- 2 treaties: Osnabruck (Emperor + Sweden + Protestant estates) & Munster (Emperor + France), both signed 24 Oct 1648; + separate Spain-Dutch peace of Munster Jan 1648 (Dutch independence recognised)
- Calvinism now recognised alongside Lutheranism and Catholicism
- "normative year" 1624 (Normaljahr): religious status of territory fixed as it was on 1 Jan 1624 -> prince can't just change his subjects' religion any more. weakens the 1555 principle a lot
- private worship allowed for minorities in some cases
- France gets most of Alsace, Sweden gets W. Pomerania + Bremen/Verden; Swiss independence confirmed
- imperial estates get right to make alliances (not against Emperor)

"WESTPHALIAN SOVEREIGNTY" ??
- IR textbooks: Westphalia = birth of the sovereign state system. Helmsley v sceptical - the treaties barely mention sovereignty, the Empire carried on until 1806, foreign powers (France, Sweden) became guarantors with a right to intervene in the Empire = opposite of non-intervention. "a 19th/20th c. myth" (cf. Osiander 2001)
- exam Q likely: "To what extent did 1648 settle the religious question in the Empire?"
