KEY INVENTIONS IN TEXTILES
- 1733: John Kay's flying shuttle doubled the speed of weaving, which created a shortage of yarn.
- c.1764: James Hargreaves, a weaver from Lancashire, invented the spinning jenny. One worker could now spin eight threads at once (later models could spin many more).
- 1769: Richard Arkwright patented the water frame, a spinning machine driven by a water wheel. Because it was too big for a cottage, it needed a purpose-built building.
- 1771: Arkwright opened Cromford Mill in Derbyshire, often called the first modern factory. Workers (many of them children) worked 13-hour shifts, and the mill ran day and night.
- 1779: Samuel Crompton's spinning mule combined features of the jenny and the water frame and produced fine, strong thread.

STEAM POWER
Thomas Newcomen built the first practical steam engine in 1712 to pump water out of mines, but it wasted a lot of fuel. James Watt added a separate condenser, which he patented in 1769, making engines far more efficient. From 1775 Watt worked in partnership with Matthew Boulton in Birmingham. By the 1780s their rotary engines could drive factory machines, so mills no longer had to be built next to fast-flowing rivers.

RAILWAYS
- 1825: The Stockton and Darlington Railway opened - the first public railway to use steam locomotives, mainly for carrying coal.
- October 1829: The Rainhill Trials were held to choose the locomotive for the new Liverpool and Manchester Railway. George and Robert Stephenson's Rocket won, reaching about 30 mph, and the Stephensons won the GBP 500 prize.
- 15 September 1830: The Liverpool and Manchester Railway opened - the first inter-city railway, with timetabled passenger trains. At the opening, the MP William Huskisson was hit by Rocket and died later that day.

FACTORY REFORM
The 1833 Factory Act:
- banned children under 9 from working in textile factories
- limited children aged 9-13 to 9 hours a day
- limited young people aged 13-18 to 12 hours a day
- required children to receive 2 hours of schooling a day
- appointed 4 factory inspectors to enforce the law (for the whole country!)
