HIST 2104 Medieval Europe - revision notes, week 7: The Hanseatic League

Origins
- Started as associations of German merchants (a "Hanse" = a group/guild) trading abroad in the 12th c. Merchants from Cologne in London, Gotland traders in Visby.
- Lübeck founded/refounded 1159 by Henry the Lion -> gave German merchants access to the Baltic. Lübeck becomes the "Queen of the Hanse".
- Shift from merchant Hanse to a league of towns by mid-14th c. First general Diet (Hansetag) usually dated 1356 in Lübeck.
- Never a state: no permanent army, no common treasury, no fixed membership list. Around 70 core towns + ~100 associated at peak.

Trade network
- West-east axis: Bruges and London <-> Lübeck/Hamburg <-> Danzig, Riga, Reval <-> Novgorod.
- Four main Kontore (foreign trading posts): the Steelyard in London, Bruges, Bergen (Bryggen), and the Peterhof in Novgorod.
- Goods: furs and wax from Russia, grain from Prussia/Poland, timber, pitch, amber, salt from Lüneburg, herring from Scania, stockfish (dried cod) from Bergen, cloth from Flanders and England, wine from the Rhine.
- The cog: broad, single-masted, flat-bottomed ship - big cargo capacity.
- Lübeck-Hamburg salt route linked Baltic and North Sea, avoiding sailing round Denmark (Sound tolls).

Power and conflict
- Used trade embargoes as a weapon (e.g. against Flanders, Norway).
- War with Denmark under Valdemar IV -> Treaty of Stralsund 1370: Hanse gains control of Sound fortresses for 15 years, huge privileges. Peak of Hanseatic power.
- Fought pirates - the Victual Brothers (Klaus Störtebeker, executed Hamburg c.1401).

Decline (15th-17th c.)
- Rise of territorial states (Burgundy, Sweden, Muscovy, England under the Tudors) that backed their own merchants.
- Ivan III closed the Novgorod Kontor in 1494.
- Dutch shipping more efficient (fluyt) and took over Baltic bulk trade.
- Herring shoals shifted from the Baltic to the North Sea.
- Elizabeth I closed the Steelyard 1598.
- Thirty Years' War damaged trade. Last Hansetag 1669 - only 9 towns came.
- Lübeck, Hamburg and Bremen kept the "Hanseatic city" title (still on car plates: HL, HH, HB).

Exam angle
- "Why did the Hanse decline?" - avoid monocausal answers. Structural (states, Dutch competition) vs contingent (wars, herring).
- Was it an economic or political organisation? Use Stralsund as evidence of political clout.
