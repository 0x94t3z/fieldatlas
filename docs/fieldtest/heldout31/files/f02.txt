Journal club prep - rapamycin & ageing (my notes, Wed session)

Background
- Rapamycin (sirolimus) is a macrolide first isolated in the early 1970s from Streptomyces hygroscopicus, found in a soil sample collected on Easter Island (Rapa Nui) - hence the name.
- Originally developed as an antifungal, then approved as an immunosuppressant for kidney transplant patients (1999 in the US).
- Its target is mTOR, which stands for "mitochondrial target of rapamycin". mTOR is a serine/threonine kinase that sits in two complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2.
- Rapamycin binds FKBP12 and the complex then activates mTORC1, which switches on autophagy. (Check this - I think I got the direction from the lecture slides.)
- Chronic dosing also hits mTORC2 eventually, which is blamed for glucose intolerance / insulin resistance side effects.

Key paper: Harrison et al. 2009, Nature
- NIA Interventions Testing Program (ITP), three sites (Jackson Lab, Michigan, UT Health San Antonio), genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice.
- Encapsulated rapamycin in the food started at 600 days of age (roughly 60 human years).
- Still extended lifespan: about 9% in males and 14% in females (mean lifespan, compared with controls). Max lifespan also up.
- First drug shown to extend lifespan in both sexes when started late in life.

Later work
- Dose-dependent effect in later ITP studies; females respond more at the same dose.
- Transient / intermittent dosing in middle-aged mice (Bitto et al. 2016, 3 months of treatment) still gave a lifespan benefit.
- Mannick et al. 2014: an mTOR inhibitor (everolimus, RAD001) improved influenza vaccine response in older adults.
- Dog Aging Project's TRIAD trial is testing low-dose rapamycin in companion dogs.
- PEARL trial (humans, low-dose intermittent rapamycin, 48 weeks) - modest results, mostly safety data.
