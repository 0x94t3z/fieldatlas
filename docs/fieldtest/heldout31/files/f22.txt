Training plan from the sports science lab (I'm 58, trying to get back into running after 12 years off)

Assessment done 12 October at the university lab, treadmill ramp test to exhaustion with gas analysis and finger-prick lactates.

Results they gave me
- VO2max 31.4 mL/kg/min. They said that is "fair" for my age and sex, roughly 45th percentile.
- Max HR measured 171 bpm. (Note: the 220-minus-age formula says 162, so the formula was wrong for me by 9 beats. They said that is normal, the formula has a standard deviation of about 10-12 beats.)
- Ventilatory threshold (VT1) at 128 bpm, respiratory compensation point (VT2 / second threshold) at 152 bpm.
- Lactate at VT2 was 3.9 mmol/L. Resting lactate 1.1.
- Running economy 215 mL/kg/km.
- Body comp: 24.1% fat, estimated fat-free mass 61 kg.
- Blood pressure response normal, no ECG abnormalities, cleared for vigorous exercise.

Zones they prescribed (5-zone, based on the thresholds not on max HR)
Z1 recovery      < 118
Z2 easy          118-128
Z3 tempo         129-144
Z4 threshold     145-152
Z5 VO2max work   153+

Weekly plan, weeks 1-6
Mon rest or 30 min walk
Tue 40 min Z2 with 6 x 20 s hill strides at the end
Wed strength: squat, hinge, single-leg, calf raises, 2 sets each, progressive
Thu 45 min Z2
Fri rest
Sat long run, start 50 min Z2 and add 5-8 min a week up to 90 min
Sun 30 min easy bike or swim
Rule they emphasised: about 80% of weekly minutes in Z1-Z2, 20% hard. Do not let the easy runs drift into Z3.

Re-test in 12 weeks. They predicted VO2max up 10-15% and said that at my age the bigger win is pushing VT2 up rather than chasing max.

Things nobody explained properly
- What VO2max actually measures and why mL/kg/min.
- What a "threshold" is and why there are two of them.
- Why running economy matters if VO2max is the headline number.
- They mentioned "VO2max declines about 10% per decade after 30 but stays higher in trained people" - is that right, and does training change the slope or just the starting point?
