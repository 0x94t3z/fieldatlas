THE TELOMERE SECRET YOUR DOCTOR WON'T TELL YOU

Every cell in your body carries a tiny clock. At the ends of your chromosomes sit telomeres - repeating stretches of DNA (TTAGGG, repeated thousands of times) that protect your genes like the plastic tips on shoelaces. Each time a cell divides, the telomeres get a little shorter. When they get too short, the cell stops dividing. Scientists call this the Hayflick limit, discovered by Leonard Hayflick in the 1960s, who found that human cells in a dish divide about 50 times before stopping.

The enzyme that rebuilds telomeres, telomerase, was discovered in 1984 by Elizabeth Blackburn and Carol Greider, working with a tiny pond organism called Tetrahymena. Their work won the Nobel Prize in 2009, shared with Jack Szostak.
