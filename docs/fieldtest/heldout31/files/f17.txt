Notes I typed while listening to the longevity podcast (ep 212, guest was some biotech founder)

Main pitch: "ageing is driven by a collapse in NAD+ levels, and if you top NAD+ back up you reverse ageing."

Claims he made, roughly in order:
1. NAD+ falls by about 50% between age 40 and 60 in every tissue, and this is the single root cause of ageing.
2. Sirtuins (SIRT1-SIRT7) need NAD+ to work. They are "the longevity genes". Resveratrol activates SIRT1 directly, which is why red wine is good for you.
3. CD38 goes up with age and eats NAD+, so an anti-CD38 drug would be the real fix.
4. NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) and NR (nicotinamide riboside) are precursors - the body converts them to NAD+ via NAMPT in the salvage pathway.
5. He said NMN is "proven to extend human lifespan" and that trials show it reverses biological age by 12 years.
6. NAD+ IV drips at a clinic are better than oral because oral NMN "doesn't survive the gut".
7. Niacin (vitamin B3) is a cheaper NAD+ precursor but causes flushing, so nobody uses it. He said flushing means it is working.
8. Sinclair's horizontal information theory of ageing: epigenetic noise, not DNA damage, is what ages us; partial reprogramming with OSK factors reversed eyesight in mice (Lu et al. 2020, optic nerve crush).
9. He claimed the FDA "banned NMN to protect pharma" in 2022.

My own notes
- He is selling an NMN product, 1 g per day, $89/month. Obvious conflict of interest.
- Mouse data vs human data got blurred constantly. He kept saying "we showed" about mouse studies and then switched to humans.
- My mum is 74 and wants to buy this. Need to know which of these claims are actually supported, which are overstated, and which are just wrong before I say anything to her.
- Also: what does "biological age" even mean here? He mentioned Horvath clocks and "DunedinPACE" without explaining either.
- He never mentioned side effects at all, which seems off for anything taken daily for years.
